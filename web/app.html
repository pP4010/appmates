Screenshots

Validate and repair store assets

Point AppMates at your app once and every other tool uses it: the rank check, the competitor comparison, the keyword builder. What the public catalogue knows about your listing is below, along with what is left to fix.

Every rule here reads the file's header, not its pixels, so a few hundred bytes answer locally what a multi-megabyte upload would have answered. That is also more correct: a fully opaque RGBA PNG is still rejected by Apple, so sampling pixels for transparency would give the wrong answer.

⬓ Drop your screenshots here or click to choose files · PNG and JPEG · nothing leaves your machine

Output is JPEG, deliberately: a canvas-exported PNG still carries a 32-bit alpha channel even when every pixel is opaque, so it would fail the very rule you came here to fix. JPEG cannot represent alpha at all, and both stores accept it. Resizing letterboxes rather than stretches — a distorted UI screenshot reads as broken to reviewers.

Apple indexes your app name, subtitle and the 100-character keyword field as one pool of words, then combines them. A phrase is reachable when each of its words is somewhere in the pool — the phrase itself never has to appear — a word already in your title is already indexed, and every space is overhead. The field is never shown to a user, so none of this is self-correcting.

Text overruns are the second most common cause of a blocked submission after malformed screenshots, and the limits differ per store — the same listing can pass Apple and fail Play. Fields above 90% of their ceiling are flagged too: translations commonly run 15–30% longer than English.

Answers what rank trackers structurally cannot: should I build this at all? They assume a live app — you cannot track the ranking of something that does not exist. This runs before the first line of code.

There is deliberately no search-volume number. Apple has never published per-keyword search counts, and the Search Ads popularity index other tools relabel as volume is an undocumented relative score whose above-floor US keyword population fell 77% in four days in September 2025. Every number here was counted.

Every ASO tool asks how hard is this keyword? as though there were one answer. There are 175 storefronts and the answer differs in each — difficulty tracks the language a term is searched in more than the size of the country. That makes it a launch decision: which locale to write first, which language to localise screenshots into.

Deliberately slow: one request per storefront against a free endpoint that rate-limits, spaced politely apart. Cached afterwards.

Who holds a term, what the field does with its screenshots, and the vocabulary everyone agrees on. Dimensions are read from the screenshot URLs themselves — the catalogue encodes the served size — so describing a whole gallery costs no bandwidth.

Screenshot availability is partial. Across a 55-app sample the catalogue exposed iPhone screenshots for 47% of apps. Apps it withheld are marked n/a rather than shown as having none, and they are excluded from the medians instead of counted as zeros. Only names and descriptions are public — subtitles and keyword fields are not — so the vocabulary below is a floor on what rivals target, not the whole of it.
This is not the App Store's ranking. The public endpoint returns results in its own relevance order, and that is what this reports — a real, repeatable signal that moves when your metadata moves. The App Store app serves search through a different path, with personalisation and paid placements, so the two can disagree. History is kept in this browser only.

Personal Play developer accounts created after 13 November 2023 need 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days before applying for production access. The word that costs people two weeks is continuously: if your count drops to 11 on day nine, Google restarts the clock.

What this isn't. Nobody here trades App Store or Play reviews, and nothing on this page ever asks a tester to leave one. Testers give you private feedback on a closed build; if a launch listing earns you real users, whether they review is entirely between them and the store. Tokens are earned only by actually testing someone else's app — they can't be bought, and only a listing's owner confirming real feedback was given can mint one.
